SUBJECT: URGENT COMPLAINT REGARDING ALLEGED DISCLOSURE VIOLATIONS, PROMOTER CLASSIFICATION CONCERNS, AND INVESTOR PROTECTION ISSUES IN DELHIVERY LIMITED - REQUEST FOR IMMEDIATE INVESTIGATION
1. INTRODUCTION

I, Aguru Manoj Kumar, Former Group-A Officer, Author and Researcher, file this complaint against Delhivery Limited ("the Company") alleging serious violations of SEBI regulations pertaining to disclosure requirements, promoter classification, and investor protection. This complaint is based on publicly available information, regulatory filings, and documented concerns that warrant immediate SEBI investigation.

The Company made its initial public offering in May 2022, raising Rs 5,235 crore at an issue price of Rs 487 per share. Since listing, the Company has consistently traded below its IPO price, causing significant losses to retail investors. The issues raised in this complaint indicate systemic regulatory violations that require SEBI's urgent intervention.

2. GROUNDS FOR COMPLAINT
2.1 PROMOTER CLASSIFICATION VIOLATION - ACCOUNTABILITY VACUUM

The most serious concern relates to the Company's promoter classification. According to the Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) filed with SEBI: "Our Company is a professionally managed company and does not have an identifiable promoter in terms of the SEBI ICDR Regulations and the Companies Act, 2013."

This classification appears to be a deliberate circumvention of SEBI's promoter accountability framework because:

  • Minimal Co-Founder Holdings: Co-founders held under 2% of the company each at listing and classified themselves as public shareholders, not promoters. This created what analysts have termed an "accountability vacuum that characterised the 2021-22 tech IPO era."
  • Avoidance of Promoter Obligations: By claiming "no identifiable promoter," the Company's original founders and key management personnel avoided:
    • Lock-in period requirements for promoter holdings
    • Enhanced disclosure obligations applicable to promoters
    • Responsibility for post-listing performance and commitments
    • Liability under SEBI's promoter-related regulations
  • Conflict of Interest: The Managing Director Sahil Barua and other key managerial personnel held significant ESOPs (Employee Stock Option Plans) worth hundreds of crores while denying promoter status. The DRHP disclosed share-based payments amounting to Rs 597.58 million charged to profit and loss accounts for key management personnel.
Key Evidence: The DRHP explicitly states the Company has "no identifiable promoter" despite having founding members who continue to exercise control through executive positions and substantial ESOP holdings.
2.2 MATERIAL DISCLOSURE LAPSES AND INVESTOR MISLEADING

At the time of its IPO in May 2022, Delhivery's DRHP and subsequent offering documents contained several concerning disclosures that appear to have misled retail investors:

A. Profitability Misrepresentation: The IPO was conducted despite the Company reporting "deeply negative" adjusted EBITDA of minus Rs 35 crore in the 9 months preceding FY22. The prospectus failed to adequately warn retail investors about the structural unprofitability of the business model, where "freight handling costs consumed 72-75% of revenues, leaving structurally limited scope for profitability."

B. Fund Utilization Concerns: The Company had "Rs 1,700 crore in cash on its balance sheet at the time of filing, yet was seeking to raise Rs 5,235 crore" with significant portions earmarked for "inorganic targets" and "general corporate purposes" that were unidentified at the time of filing.

C. Post-IPO Performance: The stock fell approximately 27% from the issue price within months of listing. As detailed in the table below, the stock has consistently traded below the IPO price of Rs 487, with a 52-week low of Rs 238.50 in April 2025, representing a decline of over 50%.

2.3 RELATED PARTY TRANSACTION DISCLOSURE DEFICIENCIES

The Company has engaged in significant related party transactions that raise governance concerns:

  • Volume of Transactions: Related party transactions aggregated to Rs 2,104.43 million representing 4.37% of revenue from contracts with customers in the nine months ended December 31, 2021.
  • Risk Disclosure: The DRHP itself admitted: "We cannot assure you these arrangements in the future, or any future related party transactions that we may enter into, individually or in the aggregate, will not have an adverse effect on our business, financial condition, results of operations, cash flows and prospects. Furthermore, any future transactions with our related parties could potentially involve conflicts of interest which may be detrimental to our Company."
  • Group Company Concerns: The Company identified Leucon Technology Private Limited as a "group company" under SEBI ICDR Regulations, indicating related party relationships that require enhanced scrutiny.
2.4 RBI COMPOUNDING FEE - REGULATORY VIOLATION

According to the Company's Business Responsibility & Sustainability Report for FY25, Delhivery paid compounding fees to the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) for "contravention under Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, where the Company has allotted equity shares after expiry of 60 days from the date of receipt of the consideration/remittance upon exercise of employee stock options."

This indicates:

  • Violation of FEMA regulations
  • Failure to maintain proper compliance frameworks
  • Potential impact on foreign investor interests
  • Inadequate internal controls over equity allotment processes
2.5 TAX VIOLATIONS - MATERIAL LITIGATION NOT ADEQUATELY DISCLOSED

The Company has received multiple tax notices indicating systemic compliance failures:

These material regulatory actions were not adequately highlighted in prospectus disclosures to warn retail investors about compliance risks.

2.6 COMPETITOR ALLEGATIONS OF MISLEADING DATA

In September 2024, Delhivery itself filed complaints with stock exchanges alleging that its rival Ecom Express "misrepresented numbers" in its DRHP regarding "shipment volumes, Service EBITDA and cost per shipment."

The Company accused Ecom Express of "double-counting RTO (return to origin) shipments" to inflate volumes. The irony is significant: Delhivery accused a competitor of the same methodological concerns about shipment counting and metric calculations that were not adequately explained in its own IPO disclosures.

2.7 INTERNAL CONTROL WEAKNESSES

The Company's FY25 Annual Report discloses: "During the year, the Company received 50 (fifty) complaints aligning sexual harassment of these, 45 were investigated and resolved, while 5 are under investigation and pending for resolution as of the end of the year."

While the Independent Auditors' Report states "no fraud by the Company and no material fraud on the Company has been noticed or reported during the year," it notes: "We have taken into consideration the whistle blower complaints received by the Company during the year."

The Company's own Whistle Blower Policy acknowledges risks including: "Financial malpractice or impropriety or fraud," "Manipulation of company data/record," and "Pilferage of confidential/proprietary information."

3. VIOLATIONS OF SEBI REGULATIONS

The above conduct appears to violate:

3.1 SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018:
  • Regulation 2(1)(pp) - Definition and obligations of promoters
  • Schedule I - Disclosure requirements in offer documents
  • Regulation 53 - Related party transaction disclosures
  • Regulation 57 - Material disclosures in prospectus
3.2 SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015:
  • Regulation 27 - Corporate governance requirements
  • Regulation 30 - Material event disclosures
  • Regulation 52 - Related party transaction compliance
  • Regulation 4 - Principles governing disclosures
3.3 SEBI (Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices) Regulations, 2003:
  • Misleading statements in prospectus
  • False or misleading information to investors
  • Failure to provide adequate risk disclosures
3.4 SEBI (Investor Protection) Guidelines:
  • Inadequate protection of retail investor interests
  • Failure to maintain adequate disclosures about business model risks
  • Promoter classification circumventing investor safeguards
4. PRAYER FOR RELIEF

In view of the above, I respectfully request SEBI to:

A. Initiate Immediate Investigation:
  • Examine the promoter classification of Delhivery Limited and determine whether the "no identifiable promoter" claim was a deliberate circumvention of SEBI regulations
  • Investigate the adequacy of IPO disclosures regarding profitability, fund utilization, and business model risks
  • Review the Company's compliance with SEBI ICDR Regulations regarding related party transactions
  • Assess whether the "general corporate purposes" fund utilization was adequately disclosed
B. Direct Enhanced Disclosures:
  • Order the Company to reclassify its original founders and key management personnel as promoters if they exercise control or significant influence
  • Mandate additional disclosures about the structural challenges in the logistics business model
  • Require detailed justification for the "general corporate purposes" utilization of IPO funds
  • Direct retrospective disclosure of all material regulatory violations
C. Protect Investor Interests:
  • Issue appropriate directions to prevent similar promoter classification circumventions in future tech IPOs
  • Direct the Company to compensate retail investors who suffered losses due to allegedly inadequate disclosures
  • Ensure strict compliance with SEBI's circular on related party transaction disclosures
  • Monitor the Company's compliance with FEMA and tax regulations going forward
D. Coordinate with Other Regulators:
  • Share findings with RBI regarding the FEMA violation that resulted in compounding fees
  • Coordinate with Ministry of Corporate Affairs regarding corporate governance concerns
  • Inform Income Tax and GST authorities regarding potential disclosure inconsistencies
5. CONCLUSION

The classification of Delhivery Limited as having "no identifiable promoter" appears to be regulatory arbitrage that deprived retail investors of the protections intended by SEBI's promoter framework. Combined with the Company's post-IPO performance, related party transaction volumes, and regulatory violations, this warrants immediate SEBI intervention to protect investor interests and maintain market integrity.

The sustained trading below IPO price (over 50% decline at its lowest point), multiple tax notices exceeding Rs 55 crore, FEMA violations, and internal control weaknesses collectively indicate systemic governance failures that require regulatory scrutiny.
