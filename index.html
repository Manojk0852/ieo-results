SUBJECT: URGENT COMPLAINT REGARDING ALLEGED DISCLOSURE VIOLATIONS, PROMOTER CLASSIFICATION CONCERNS, AND INVESTOR PROTECTION ISSUES IN DELHIVERY LIMITED - REQUEST FOR IMMEDIATE INVESTIGATION
1. INTRODUCTION

I, Aguru Manoj Kumar, Former Group-A Officer, Author and Researcher, file this complaint against Delhivery Limited ("the Company") alleging serious violations of SEBI regulations pertaining to disclosure requirements, promoter classification, and investor protection.

The Company made its initial public offering in May 2022, raising Rs 5,235 crore at an issue price of Rs 487 per share. Since listing, the Company has consistently traded below its IPO price, causing significant losses to retail investors.

2. GROUNDS FOR COMPLAINT
2.1 PROMOTER CLASSIFICATION VIOLATION - ACCOUNTABILITY VACUUM

The Company claimed in its DRHP: “Our Company is a professionally managed company and does not have an identifiable promoter...” This appears to be a deliberate circumvention of SEBI’s promoter accountability framework.

2.2 MATERIAL DISCLOSURE LAPSES AND INVESTOR MISLEADING

The IPO was conducted despite deeply negative adjusted EBITDA. Retail investors were not adequately warned about structural unprofitability and business model risks.

2.3 RELATED PARTY TRANSACTION DISCLOSURE DEFICIENCIES

Related party transactions aggregated to Rs 2,104.43 million (4.37% of revenue) with admitted risks of conflicts of interest.

2.4 RBI COMPOUNDING FEE & TAX VIOLATIONS

The Company paid compounding fees to RBI for FEMA violations and received multiple GST and Income Tax notices totalling over Rs 55 crore.

2.5 COMPETITOR ALLEGATIONS OF MISLEADING DATA

Delhivery itself accused Ecom Express of inflating numbers while similar concerns exist in its own disclosures.

3. VIOLATIONS OF SEBI REGULATIONS

The conduct appears to violate SEBI (ICDR) Regulations 2018, SEBI (LODR) Regulations 2015, and SEBI (PFUTP) Regulations 2003.

4. PRAYER FOR RELIEF

I request SEBI to immediately investigate, examine promoter classification, order enhanced disclosures, protect retail investors, and coordinate with other regulators.

5. CONCLUSION

The “no identifiable promoter” classification combined with sustained losses, regulatory violations, and governance concerns warrants urgent SEBI intervention to protect market integrity.
